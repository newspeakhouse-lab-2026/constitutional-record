# Preference mapping — the Emys Polis

*The Layer 3 process (Constitution §2) asks the proposer to run a preference-mapping step before the vote. This is the record of it: an open [Polis](https://pol.is) conversation mapping where the cohort agrees and disagrees about Emys, before anyone is asked to prefer, tolerate, or object. It is not operative.*

**Live conversation: [https://pol.is/3pcbaxnrvt](https://pol.is/3pcbaxnrvt)**

**Where it stands.** 4 participants, 82 votes, 2 of them adding statements of their own. That is a thin sample of a 14-member cohort — enough to satisfy the Layer 3 step, not enough to read as consensus, and nothing here should be cited as evidence that the Laboratory agrees about anything.

## Framing — for the top of the Polis

> **Emys — mapping where we stand.** Emys is a proposed community AI for the Lab. For a two-month experiment it would read our shared channels into a searchable memory, give each of us a private AI workspace, send a personalised digest of what concerns us, and connect read-only to the house's tools (booking, library, calendar, the Record). It is run by one member on a server he rents, not by the Laboratory, which pays the cost and sets the terms. The full proposal — policy, rationale, privacy declaration — is in `policies/community-ai/`.
>
> This Polis is not the vote. It is here to surface what we actually agree and disagree on, so the proposal can be shaped before it is put to the cohort. Vote **agree / disagree / pass** on each statement, and **add your own**. The aim is to find the real clusters of opinion, not to win an argument.

## Two statements whose results cannot be read as support

The proposal changed while this conversation was running. It had Emys on the Laboratory's own compute under two administrators; it now has one operator on a server he rents, with inference bought from the East London GPU Coop. **Two of the original statements were written against that earlier description, and were voted on before it changed:**

- **"I'm comfortable with Emys reading our shared channels, given it runs on our own compute and sends nothing to an outside company."** A conditional whose condition does not hold. An agreement means *"I am comfortable given X"* where X is not the case, which says nothing about comfort under the actual arrangement.
- **"I'm willing to trust two named administrators not to look at my private messages, even though they could technically access them."** The real question is one person whom nobody else can check. Two administrators watching each other is a materially softer ask, so agreement here overstates support for what is proposed.

**Neither has been removed.** Deleting them would discard votes members actually cast, and Polis moderation is for abuse and duplicates rather than for a world that moved. They stay in the conversation; sharper versions of both have been added beside them, at 11 and 15 below. **When the results are read, those two originals do not count as support for this proposal.**

A third original — *"Running Emys on our own compute, rather than an external API, matters to me"* — stands unchanged and is **more** informative than when it was written: it asks what a member values, and the proposal does not self-host, so anyone who agreed with it has a stated reason to object.

## Statements

Short, single-idea, and deliberately spread across the real tensions — including the ones against. Polis shows them one at a time, in random order; members add more.

**Finding things today**

1. I often struggle to find things I know are somewhere in our Discord.
2. I frequently miss things posted in our channels that were relevant to me.
3. I usually don't know who in the cohort could help with what I'm working on.
4. The real problem is that our channels are badly organised, not that search is hard.

**Whether Emys would help**

5. I'd use an AI assistant that could find things across our channels and house systems.
6. Knowing what others are working on would help me find collaborators.
7. A daily digest of what I care about would be useful to me.
8. I would actually do my own project work inside a shared AI workspace.
9. I'm not convinced Emys solves a problem I actually have.
10. Tools like this get built and then nobody uses them.

**Privacy and trust**

11. I'm comfortable with Emys reading our shared channels, given it stores only attributed facts and never opinions, complaints or anything personal.
12. Knowing Emys stores facts from our channels would make me more guarded about what I post.
13. Leaving opinions and complaints out of the shared memory would be enough to protect my privacy.
14. It reassures me that my private messages and workspace can never enter the shared community memory.
15. I'm willing to trust one member, who holds the server, not to read my private messages, even though he technically could and no one else can check.
16. If Emys later connects people by what they're working on, I'd want to approve each thing about me before it's shared.
17. If the operator asked, I'd be happy for how I use Emys to be analysed to improve the tool.
18. I'd rather two people administered Emys and checked each other, even if that meant the proposer's code had to be readable by someone else.
19. Taking a whole channel out of Emys should need a vote of the Lab, not just one member asking.

**Openness and control**

20. It's enough that Emys's model is open-weights and its prompts are open, even if the orchestration engine stays the proposer's closed code.
21. The Laboratory should only run AI whose every part is open-source, even if that rules Emys out.
22. It matters to me that our messages are processed in the UK and go to no commercial AI company, even though they pass through a co-op's GPUs.
23. Emys should never gain write access to our tools without another vote.
24. I'm uncomfortable that the Lab would depend on software it cannot read or fork.

**What it costs**

25. About £69 a month is a reasonable price for this experiment.
26. A 20% rise in our recurring costs is too much for a two-month trial.
27. It changes things for me that the £50 of compute is available for my own work too, not just for Emys.
28. I'd rather this money went somewhere else.

**Is it worth it**

29. A time-limited experiment is a sensible way to find out whether this is worth it.
30. I'd rather we spent the effort organising our Discord than building an AI for it.

## Statements members added

One, and it is worth reading closely:

- **A weekly digest of what I care about would be useful to me.**

A member took the seeded statement about a *daily* digest and wrote the same sentence with **weekly** in place of daily. That is a preference about frequency, offered without being asked for, and it now bears directly on clause 1.2, which authorises a personalised digest without fixing how often it arrives. The clause lets a member turn the digest off; it does not let them slow it down.
