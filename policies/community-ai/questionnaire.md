# Emys — before/after questionnaire

*A companion to the Community AI policy, not operative. It is the self-report half of the experiment's evidence (clause 9.2), alongside Emys's usage record and the evidence that it informed something the Laboratory actually did.*

**How it is run.** Anonymous, in the governance channel or a form. The first seven items run **unchanged before Emys ships** (the baseline — it cannot be reconstructed later) and **again near the end** (around 20 January 2027). The three post-only items run at the end alone. Keep the scales, labels, order, and wording identical between the two rounds.

**Scales.**
- *Frequency* (behaviour): Never · Once · A few times · Many times · (N/A)
- *Agreement* (attitude): Strongly disagree · Disagree · Neither · Agree · Strongly agree · (N/A)

Every item offers **N/A / "haven't used this"**, so non-users are not pushed into agreeing. On items 1–7 an N/A can also mean a member was simply not around that week; read it as missing rather than as a low score.

**Item 7 was added on 12 November 2026**, after the first two baseline responses were collected on 6 November. Those two have no baseline for it and were not asked to resubmit, so item 7's before-and-after pair runs on fewer people than items 1–6.

## Items — before and after (identical)

1. **Findability** *(frequency)* — In the past week, how often did you fail to find something you knew was somewhere in our Discord?
2. **Missed information** *(frequency)* — In the past week, how often did you discover you had missed something posted that was relevant to you?
3. **Keeping up** *(agreement)* — I feel on top of what's happening across the Laboratory's channels.
4. **Interest-matching** *(agreement)* — When something matching my interests is posted, I reliably come across it.
5. **People-matching** *(agreement)* — I know which members have expertise in specific areas. *(adapted from the "specialization" subscale of the [Lewis 2003 transactive-memory system scale](https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-9010.88.4.587))*
6. **Effort** *(agreement)* — Finding what I need in our channels takes a lot of effort.
7. **Knowing what's on** *(frequency)* — In the past week, how often did you find out about something happening at the House too late to go, or only by chance?

## Items — after only

8. **Usefulness** *(agreement)* — I find Emys useful. *(adapted from the perceived-usefulness construct, [Davis 1989 / TAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology_acceptance_model))*
9. **Usefulness** *(agreement)* — Emys makes it easier to find what I need at the House. *(adapted from [Davis 1989 / TAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology_acceptance_model))*
10. **Intention** *(agreement)* — I intend to keep using Emys over the coming weeks. *(adapted from behavioural intention, [Venkatesh et al. 2003, UTAUT](https://doi.org/10.2307/30036540))*

**Open text** (after only): *Anything else about finding information, or finding people to work with, in the Lab?*

## Reading the results

- **Triangulate, don't isolate:** the questionnaire is a directional signal; the usage record is the hard data; Emys having informed something the Laboratory actually did is the strongest revealed preference.
- At n≈14 this is **not** inferential statistics. Report the shift per item and per person; if a test is wanted, a paired non-parametric one (Wilcoxon signed-rank) fits. Do not lean on p-values.
- Items 1, 2, 6 and 7 should move *down* if Emys works; 3, 4, 5 *up*.

*Note: items 5, 8, 9 and 10 are **adapted** — reworded for a volunteer fellowship — from the [Lewis (2003)](https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-9010.88.4.587), [Davis (1989)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology_acceptance_model) and [Venkatesh et al. (2003)](https://doi.org/10.2307/30036540) scales, not reproduced verbatim: the original workplace wording (e.g. "improve my job performance") does not fit here. The sources are linked for lineage; the exact validated items live in each paper's appendix if you would rather use them literally.*
