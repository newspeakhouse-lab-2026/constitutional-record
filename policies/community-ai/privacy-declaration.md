# Emys — privacy declaration

*A summary of the Community AI policy for members. Not operative: where this and the policy differ, the policy governs. The numbers in brackets are the policy's clauses.*

**Who runs it.** Not the Laboratory. One member, **@emergentvibe**, runs Emys on a server he rents (3.1, 11.1). The Laboratory pays the costs and sets the terms he must run it on; it holds no administrative access to the machine (3.1).

**What that means for your data.** He can technically reach everything stored on that machine, and no other member can check what he does there. He undertakes not to read your workspace, your messages with Emys, or your personal memory, except to investigate a bug or incident someone has reported (10.1(b)). That is a promise with a remedy behind it, not a technical barrier — the policy says so in as many words. If your content is ever accessed without authorisation, or lost, he must tell the Laboratory without delay (10.1(h)).

**What it reads and keeps.** The shared Discord channels, and the Laboratory's instruments read-only (1.2, 7.1). From the channels it stores only attributed facts — events, schedules, logistics, resources, decisions — never opinions, arguments, complaints about people, or anyone's health, relationships or circumstances (6.1, 6.2). Your workspace and your messages with Emys stay private and never enter the shared memory (5.1, 5.2).

**What it was started with.** The Laboratory's and the programme's own public documents (6.6). The existing shared-channel messages, under the same limits as live ones (6.4), with personal-feed channels left out unless their owner opted in. And the **topic.forum profiles** of members, and of Faculty who have joined the Discord, which those people wrote and published themselves — each entry names its source, and yours comes out of the shared memory if you ask the operator (6.7). Emys also holds a map of who is who — Discord IDs, display names, personal channel IDs, GitHub handles — in a private repository visible to the cohort.

**Where it goes.** Stored and processed in the United Kingdom. The open-weights models that answer you run on the **East London GPU Coop's** GPUs, which keeps no logs — the coop's word, which the Laboratory cannot verify from outside. Nothing goes to a commercial AI provider; nothing is used to train or evaluate a model, sold, published, or used for any other community or client (8.1, 10.1(c), 10.1(d)).

**Two things no clause can fix, so they are said plainly.** You reach Emys through Discord, so **your messages to Emys and its replies pass through Discord**, a US company that can read them and holds them on its own terms. That is already true of everything you do on the server. And the **web interface is served through the Laboratory's own server** (3.1), so requests to it pass through Laboratory infrastructure, where those who administer that server could see them. Neither route is private from everyone.

**What you can do on your own, with no vote and no reason given.**

- **Have your own content removed from Emys, in whole or in part, at any time** (10.4).
- Correct or delete your own personal memory by asking Emys directly — no request to anyone (8.2).
- Keep a personal-feed channel out unless you opt in (6.3).
- Have your topic.forum profile taken out of the shared memory, on request to the operator (6.7).
- Decline any analysis of how you personally use Emys — it needs your opt-in, and you can withdraw it (8.4).

**What takes a decision of the Laboratory.** Excluding a whole shared channel from Emys is an **Ordinary resolution**, which any member may propose; on exclusion, whatever was already taken from that channel comes out of the shared memory (6.3). You never need one to protect your own material — 10.4 above is yours alone.

**When it ends.** 29 January 2027, or with the Constitution if that is sooner. The shared memory is copied at least monthly to the **Laboratory's shared Google Drive**, readable without his software, so it survives both the experiment and him (10.6). On expiry he deletes members' content and confirms it in the governance channel (10.7).
