Dispatches Newsletter Management - Policy Proposal
Sep 29, 2026 · Proposed by Madison Karas
This is a policy proposal, subject to the Constitution's 7-day review process (see Adoption & Amendment).

Purpose & Scope
This policy governs Dispatches: the Laboratory's weekly newsletter to faculty, the fellowship, and other acquaintances of Newspeak House, to which the Charter's Findings provisions require. It also assumes the newsletter, the main site, and subscriber management will live on one platform rather than three separate tools (platform(s) are determined in separate proposal(s)). It sets out how the Laboratory gathers what is happening, decides what is worth publishing, and keeps the system running without it collapsing onto the same one or two people every week.
It does not cover the Laboratory's other public channels except where they intersect with weekly or site publication, nor does it set the Laboratory's overall editorial line on external topics — only the process by which its own story gets told. It covers who looks after the site day to day (see Roles & Rotation), not which tool that site runs on, and not what non-newsletter content goes on it beyond one suggestion: a page for Fellowship Candidates biographies.

Gathering Material
During the submission window, anyone can add material two ways: writing straight into the shared drafting doc themselves, or posting it as a message in #dispatches tagged ADDITION — either way, it's there for the Editor to pull from when the week's issue comes together.
The channel is the raw feed, not the newsletter. Nothing in it is published unedited — the Editor (see Roles) treats it as material to be gathered and synthesised, per the Charter's expectation that dispatches cover experiments, visitors, fieldnotes, and member activities, etc., and not a live chat log.

Editorial Process
At the start of their term, the Editor sets that week's shape: a short prompt posted to #dispatches — what sections this issue needs, and suggestions which items from the intake feed are worth featuring. The format does not need to be the same every week, it is intended to be experimental. The shared drafting doc opens Thursday morning and stays open through Sunday night; that's the submission window, and anyone can write straight into the structure the Editor laid out, not just flag things for someone else to write up.
Typical sections — though the Editor decides what actually runs each week, and there's no fixed format; each week can take whatever shape the Editor sees fit:
weekly updates from fellows, in their own words, on whatever they want to share
recaps of Laboratory events and experiments
visitor interviews
commentary on trends, events in public discourse from fellows
whatever else fits 
Monday and Tuesday are the Editor's own: assembling, cutting, and writing the transitions. Voice stays first person plural and concrete over promotional — as willing to report a stalled experiment or an open conflict as a clean win, since the Laboratory's governance arrangements are exactly the findings the State of the House Address already commits it to publishing.
AI tools may help the Editor pull a first pass together from the week's intake, but the editorial judgment — what runs, what it says, the voice — stays theirs; this role isn't meant to be replaced by an aggregation script. Any AI use should follow any Laboratory's AI use policy (if it exists), and common sense about privacy in the meantime.
Before the edition goes out, the Publisher and the Agent both read the draft on Tuesday: the Publisher as a second pair of eyes on the copy, the Agent because Dispatches goes out under the Laboratory's name. That Tuesday read is also the last checkpoint for objections — if a member flags that something written about them shouldn't run as written, it is held until resolved, never published over an objection.
Disputes about whether an item should run at all, as opposed to how it's framed, go to the ordinary proposal process — not to the Editor, Publisher, or Agent individually.

Cadence
Dispatches launches Wednesday, 14 October 2026, and publishes every Wednesday morning after that — timed to land before that evening's Ration Club, so it can double as that week's conversation-starter and an easy way to recruit new subscribers in person. Anyone may subscribe, not only faculty and fellowship, though they remain the primary audience.
Whatever platform the Laboratory settles on — tool selection is a separate proposal — needs to send the weekly issue to subscriber and support functionality for list management and donations or paid subscriptions, , and host an archive plus publishing capability  for longer material that doesn't fit inside a Wednesday issue: full interviews, write-ups, other Laboratory publications, and as a suggestion, a page of names and short bios for the 14 fellows.

Roles & Rotation
Newsletter labour splits into two rotating roles, each fellow holding each exactly once over the course of the Charter. Once the proposal passes, the first editor will facility a sign-up for fellow candidates to claim a space in the publishing schedule. Claiming a round as Editor also claims you as the previous round's Publisher — one sign-up fills both, so there's never a separate scheduling clash between the two:
Editor — sets the week's structure, runs the Thursday–Sunday submission window, and assembles and writes the issue for review.
Publisher — the round right before your own Editor term: an onboarding period. That week's Editor focuses purely on the newsletter; the Publisher reviews each issue alongside the Agent before it goes out, does the actual publishing, and handles the rest of the site — any other articles or pages, upkeep of the subscriber list, promotion of Laboratory events through the newsletter and site. By the time your own Editor term starts, the tools and the site are already familiar. Income from the tip jar and any subscriptions is managed jointly with the Treasurer, per Treasury's own policy, rather than by the Publisher alone.
Each term runs three weeks — long enough to settle in without becoming a burden nobody wants twice. To take a round, a fellow candidate will write into that round's Editor cell in the table above — doing so also fills you in as the previous round's Publisher, since claiming an Editor term is what creates the onboarding commitment the round before it.  First come, first served. Round 1 is the exception: it starts cold, with no round before it to shadow — and in exchange, they close the loop by also becoming Round 14's Publisher, so every claimed round still ends up with both roles filled exactly once. Any two fellows may still swap after claiming, by mutual agreement.
If a round's Editor slot is still unclaimed a week before it's due to start, anyone in the Laboratory may claim it on short notice, or that round's already-claimed Publisher may simply run it solo rather than leave an issue unpublished. Better a slightly informal handoff than a missed issue.
If an Editor or Publisher goes silent mid-term with no notice, the other role-holder may publish alone rather than let an issue lapse; a repeated pattern is a matter for the ordinary conflict process, not this policy.

Record-Keeping
The published archive, not the intake channel, is the record that counts if a dispatch is ever cited in a dispute or audit — per the Rule of Recognition, it needs to be durable, inspectable, and tamper-evident, which a chat log is not. Where a dispatch touches a decision or judgment rather than just news, the Laboratory's constitutional record is the authoritative copy, not the newsletter.

Evaluation
Open-rate, subscriber-growth, and tip-jar numbers are tracked from the first issue and reviewed monthly by the current Publisher, whose job the site already is — not chased weekly, since a single low-open week or a quiet donation month says little on its own.
If a round's Editor and Publisher both go quiet with no notice, the following round's pair may start early rather than let issues lapse — how the missed weeks get made up, if at all, is for the Laboratory to sort out when it actually happens, not something worth over-specifying here.

Adoption & Amendment
This is submitted as a policy resolution under the Two-door rule, not an ordinary rule — it creates new roles, a rotation, and a financial arrangement, which counts as redesigning governance for an area rather than day-to-day rule-making. Its process: at least one other member endorses it, the proposer conducts a preference-mapping step, then seven days of deliberation. Voting is "prefer" / "tolerate" / "object" — prefer counts as affirmative support, tolerate as abstention, object as opposition — and adoption needs affirmative support from a majority of all members, at least 8 of the Laboratory's 14, regardless of how many actually vote.
The cadence, term length, and rotation mechanism above are proposed defaults, not fixed commitments — they're expected to change once the Laboratory has a few months of real data on what's actually sustainable. Like any policy, this one is amended the same way it was adopted, and is reviewed no later than the Interim Constitution's own expiry date.
