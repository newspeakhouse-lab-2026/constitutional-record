# Rationale — Co-Safety Leads for the current term

Reasoning only. Nothing here is operative; the rule is
`rule-co-safety-leads.md`.

## Why a separate instrument rather than the policy itself

The safeguarding and wellbeing Policy (#21) names nobody. §8 says "Names and
contacts live in the Quick reference", and the Quick reference row for Co-Safety
Leads links to `roles.md`. §8 then says the roles "are filled through the
ordinary rule process at the start of each term".

So the Policy creates the office and deliberately declines to fill it. Filling it
is a separate act, and §8 says which act: the ordinary rule process.

Putting the names into #21 instead would have meant two problems. The eight
members who recorded a preference on #21 voted on a policy that appoints nobody,
so adding named holders would be a material change under §2 and would restart the
seven-day period on a proposal whose window had already closed. And `roles.md`
states that "a role is held only if it was conferred through the Constitution's
procedures" and that "a commit does not confer one" — so names typed into the
file would have recorded an office nobody held.

## Why Layer 2 and not Layer 3

Layer 3 covers creating an office. This creates none: the office exists because
the Policy creates it. This operates within that frame and sets who holds it,
which is Layer 2.

The rule instrument template warns that creating an office makes something a
Policy. That warning is about the creating act, not the filling act, and §8 is
explicit that filling happens through the ordinary rule process — the Policy
would contradict itself if filling it required another Policy.

## Why Tier A

48 hours, passing absent a stated objection. The office is currently vacant and
the Policy routes every safety concern to it, so the cost of a slower tier is a
live policy with nobody to receive a concern under it.

Tier A costs nothing if that judgement is wrong: one stated objection moves it to
Tier B, and the objection is the point rather than an obstacle.

## Why these two

Not a matter for this file. The proposing member brings the names; the cohort's
48 hours is where they are tested. What belongs here is what the instrument
should not be read to decide:

- It confers no authority beyond §8 of the Policy.
- It does not make either holder a deputy to the other, because §8 creates two
  co-equal Co-Safety Leads rather than a lead and a deputy. The rows currently on
  the #21 branch say "Safety Lead" and "Deputy Safety Lead", which is a different
  office; PR #102 corrects that.

## The term, and why it is short

§8 sets two months. §5 expires the Constitution at 23:59 UK on Monday 30 November
2026 unless it is re-ratified or replaced, and no office outlasts the Constitution
that creates it — `roles.md` already says so for every other office.

As the Record stands, that makes this appointment a short one. §5 also requires
the final re-ratification or replacement proposal to have entered its seven-day
deliberation period no later than Monday 23 November 2026. That is a separate
matter from this rule and is not for this file to resolve, but the term stated
here is the honest consequence of it, and the `Ends:` line says so rather than
implying two months the Constitution may not be there to support.
