## Why this particular lead pairing

Fogg's B=MAP model (behaviour = motivation × ability × prompt, multiplicative,
not additive — verified against his own published framework) gives the
"disengaged member" seat something sharper to argue from than general
sympathy: it predicts *exactly* where the current page fails, because ability
cost (parsing an 8-clause run-on, finding your name in an unsorted list,
hand-editing a markdown table) is high in several identifiable places, and a
multiplicative model says a single high-friction step kills the whole
action regardless of how motivated the member is. Norman's affordance/slip
framework explains *why* those steps are high-friction in design terms
(competing-weight typography, a CTA styled as prose, a 60-char run-on with
no visual stops) rather than just asserting that they are. Putting them in
the same two seats means most of the transcript can stay concrete — pointing
at a line number and a CSS rule — rather than staying abstract.

## Why the other five are not cut, only quieted

Cutting them would have lost the one connective finding the panel actually
produced: the dashboard's single most important link routes to the *harder*
path (raw file edit) rather than the validated issue form — and that this
is simultaneously a UI problem (Fogg/Norman: more friction than necessary for
everyone), a security/audit problem (Schneier: the form's commit carries a
fixed message distinguishing transcription from decision; a hand-edit carries
none unless the member writes it themselves), a legitimacy problem (Elster:
weaker evidentiary standing for a vote recorded the harder way), and an
access problem that lands hardest on exactly the two members without push
access (Scott) — who are also the two Constitution §1 names as needing a
"reasonably equivalent route." No single lens would have produced that
whole picture; the UI-primary framing just means it is *introduced* by
Fogg and Norman noticing the symptom, and the other five are asked what it
costs, not asked to run their own parallel inquiry.
