# Expert selection — Panel 1 (Design Critique)

See `planning.md` for the full reasoning. Summary table:

| Expert | Seat | Real source grounding this voice draws on |
|---|---|---|
| Don Norman | Interaction / affordances | *The Design of Everyday Things*, rev. ed. 2013 — "signifiers," error as system design failure, not user failure |
| Dieter Rams | Restraint / critic of addition | Vitsœ's published "ten principles for good design"; "as little design as possible" |
| Edward Tufte | Information design | *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, 1983 — data-ink ratio, graphical integrity, lie factor |
| B.J. Fogg | Behaviour design | "A Behavior Model for Persuasive Design," Persuasive 2009 (B=MAT); *Tiny Habits*, 2019 (B=MAP, trigger renamed prompt). Returning from `../record-votes-panel/panels/panel-01-critical.md` |
| George Orwell | Prose / plain language | "Politics and the English Language," 1946 — six concrete rules, tested directly against the page's sentences |
| Jakob Nielsen | Usability / empirical critic | "How Users Read on the Web," Alertbox, Oct 1997; "F-Shaped Pattern for Reading Web Content," Alertbox, Apr 2006 (eyetracking, n=232) |
| Christopher Alexander | Wholeness / pattern language | *The Timeless Way of Building*, 1979 ("the quality without a name"); *A Pattern Language*, 1977 |

No one invented from whole cloth; no one asked to argue a position their real
work contradicts. Fogg is the one returning voice — justified in planning.md
as continuity on the exact question ("what would make a member open it
voluntarily") he is already on record about, not padding.

## Who was considered and not selected

- **Jony Ive / Luke Wroblewski** — industrial design and mobile-input
  specialists respectively; this page has no hardware surface and no novel
  input pattern (it is links, a dialog, and text), so neither seat earns its
  place over the two above.
- **Marty Cagan / Julie Zhuo** — product-process voices. The brief is about
  the artefact, not the team or roadmap that produced it; out of scope by the
  brief's own first sentence.
- **A second behaviourist to argue against Fogg** — considered, rejected:
  Rams and Nielsen between them already supply the "cut it" pressure Fogg's
  "add a trigger" proposals need tested against, and a panel this size with
  two behaviourists would crowd out Alexander's and Orwell's distinct seats.

## Expected fault lines (written before the transcript, so they can be checked against what actually happened)

1. Rams vs. Fogg on whether the page should ever *add* anything to move
   behaviour, or only ever get out of the way.
2. Tufte vs. Norman on the gauge bar — data-ink minimalism vs. an affordance
   that may need more signifying than a bare filled arc.
3. Nielsen as a standing veto on any proposal that adds reading, regardless of
   who proposes it.
4. Alexander against the table's working assumption that "clearer" and
   "better" are the same axis.
